# Data notes

How this prototype treats sources that do not agree. The organizers' data list is the citation source for names, URLs, and caveats. `data/processed/sources.json` is the machine-readable copy of what was actually pulled. Most layers were pulled on 2026-09-26. The CHAS tract file, the three WPRDC site-inventory layers, HUD LIHTC and QCT, ACS B25003, and the 2015–2019 rent file were pulled on 2026-09-27.

Sources disagree on geography, time, and definition. This build does not average those disagreements into one number. Each score input keeps the geography and definition it came from. A missing source is left missing.

## What is joined to a parcel

The parcel polygon is the Allegheny County GIS parcel, clipped to the official city neighborhood polygons for Hazelwood, Lower Lawrenceville, Central Lawrenceville, and Upper Lawrenceville. Assignment uses the neighborhood with the largest overlap. A parcel is kept when its representative point is inside that neighborhood, or when the overlap is at least half the parcel.

## Mismatches we did not paper over

**Site-inventory records versus the parcel map.** The three WPRDC layers are keyed by the county parcel ID and joined exactly; nothing is matched by address. In this pull 986 of the 8,645 parcels are City-owned (436 of those are greenways or parks), 1,491 are on the City delinquency list (1,164 for a prior year too), 190 are condemned or dead-end, and 1,911 are vacant land by the county land use or the City's class (390 are unknown because neither source has a row). A parcel ID missing from a list is recorded as "no", because each list is the full citywide file. If a layer fails to download, its field is null ("unknown") and the Find Sites filter for it matches nothing. The lists update on different schedules (City-owned 2026-09-26, delinquency 2026-09-24, condemned 2026-09-26) and are not reconciled with each other. Twelve parcels are both City-owned and on the delinquency list. The app shows both flags and does not decide which record is current.

**Vacancy.** The county land use and the City's class can disagree. The vacant flag is true if either says vacant land. `vacant_land_use` keeps the county reading alone. Neither is a site visit.

**Displacement inputs: three vintages, two tract vintages.** Renter share is 2020–2024 ACS. Low-income renter cost burden is 2018–2022 CHAS. The rent change compares 2015–2019 with 2020–2024. Those two 5-year periods do not overlap, which is the Census Bureau's guidance for comparing 5-year estimates. The 2021 table-based file would have been easier to read, but it overlaps 2020–2024 by two years. 2015–2019 estimates use 2010 tracts. The 2020–2010 tract relationship file decides what can be compared. A 2020 tract uses its own 2010 tract when the GEOIDs match and each covers at least 95% of the other's land area (357 Allegheny tracts). It uses a "parent" 2010 tract when it lies at least 95% inside one 2010 tract (17 more, usually splits). Otherwise rent pressure is missing. In the MVP, Hazelwood's tract 42003562901 is a split of 42003562900. Its 2015–2019 rent ($911) describes the larger parent tract, and the parcel's confidence notes say so. Rents are nominal. Subtracting the county's change ($890 to $1,153, +29.6%) removes region-wide inflation and market movement. Tract medians carry wide margins of error, so a few points of difference should not be read as real.

**Displacement screen versus the equity score.** Equity already subtracts a displacement penalty based on neighborhood sale prices and turnover (market heat). The displacement screen is a separate tract measure of renter vulnerability and rent pressure. They overlap in intent. Both stay visible, and their weights are independent.

**Carbon: published figures versus chosen tiers.** The RECS 2020 figures are published: Table CE1.2, Northeast census region, average annual site energy per household (single-family detached 120.7, single-family attached 85.4, apartments in 2–4 unit buildings 68.0, apartments in 5+ unit buildings 36.2 million Btu; https://www.eia.gov/consumption/residential/data/2020/c&e/xls/ce1.2.xlsx). They cover existing homes of every age, measure site energy rather than emissions, and do not separate fuel mix. They are used only as ratios to single-family detached. Townhouse / duplex uses the attached figure. A small apartment (3–19 units) uses the 2–4 unit figure, which is the conservative end. A large apartment uses 5+ units. Embodied carbon has no published per-home number that fits these four types in Pittsburgh. Zuluaga & Saxe (2025, Environ. Res.: Infrastruct. Sustain. 5 035015, https://doi.org/10.1088/2634-4505/adfc95) report city medians around 79,000–102,000 kgCO2e per new single-unit dwelling against about 39,000–60,000 for new units in buildings adding more than 10 units. Rankin, Arceo, Isin & Saxe (2024, Journal of Industrial Ecology 28(3):455–468, https://doi.org/10.1111/jiec.13461) find low-rise multi-unit buildings lower per bedroom than single-family and mid/high-rise, with more variation within forms than between them. So the embodied part is a relative tier: 1.0 for single-family and 0.6 for all three multi-unit types. That is a choice, labeled as such in `score_model.json`. The transport part is 100 minus the transit score. No vehicle-miles or emissions figure is claimed for it.

**Parcel geometry and the assessment roll.** 411 of 8,645 clipped parcels (about 5%) had no assessment row. The organizers' caveat is that those two files update on different schedules. Those parcels have lower confidence and blank land-use and sale fields.

**Neighborhood sales versus block-group census.** Demand uses neighborhood sale prices. Equity uses block-group income and ACS rent burden, compared with the county median, plus the CHAS tract share described below. A block group can cross a neighborhood line, and a neighborhood contains many block groups. The two are not averaged into one "neighborhood condition."

**ACS versus HUD CHAS.** Both are in the equity score, and they are not the same number. ACS B25070 (2024 5-year, survey years 2020–2024) is the share of all renter households with a computed rent-to-income ratio who pay 30% or more, usually at the block group. CHAS Table 8 (2018–2022, released December 2025) is the share of renter households at or below 80% of HAMFI who pay more than 30% of income, at the census tract, among households whose cost burden was computed. CHAS lags the ACS file by several years: the special tabulation ends in 2022, and the ACS tables used here run through 2024. A tract is also larger than a block group, and the 2024 block-group boundaries are a newer vintage than the CHAS geography. The need score gives equal weight to the income gap, the ACS share, and the CHAS share when each is present. A missing CHAS cell is left missing. It is not treated as zero, and the two shares are not averaged into one unlabeled "rent burden." Column names are the 2018–2022 dictionary: renter bands `T8_est69`, `T8_est82`, and `T8_est95`, with cost-burden subtotals `T8_est73`+`T8_est76`, `T8_est86`+`T8_est89`, and `T8_est99`+`T8_est102`. The file is `2018thru2022-140-csv.zip` from the CHAS download page.

**Assessed value versus sale price.** Assessed value is not market value. It is not requested and not scored. Demand uses valid sale prices. The separate Allegheny County sale-transactions dataset was not downloaded (the list URL returned HTTP 404; the live WPRDC page is `real-estate-sales`). The validation-code filter from that list row is what the assessment sale fields go through. Nominal transfers are excluded. A sale price is still not an appraisal.

**Zoning map versus zoning code.** The map supplies `zon_new` only. Permissions are read from Pittsburgh Zoning Code §911.02, the use table at https://ecode360.com/45476524#45476524, saved from the code page after ecode360 had blocked an earlier unattended request. Legend, from §911.01F: P is permitted by right, A is an administrator exception, S is a special exception, C is a conditional use, and a blank cell is not permitted. Subdistricts use the base column (R1D-VL uses R1D, GT-A uses GT, RIV-MU uses the RIV MU column). The header's `startcol` attributes were checked. Special is P, H, EMI. DT is GT plus one unlabeled column that is blank for these residential rows and is not mapped to a district. RIV starts at RM, then MU, NS, GI, IMU. That matches the residential CSV.

The four housing types are an assumption, not categories in the code. Single-family is Single-Unit Detached. Townhouse / duplex is Single-Unit Attached or Two-Unit. A small apartment is Three-Unit when it is 3 units and Multi-Unit when it is 4–19. A large apartment (20 or more units) is Multi-Unit. Where those rows disagree, the screen says so (for example, triplex allowed and 4+ units not). In R1D, Single-Unit Attached is P/S under §911.04A.69A: by right on lots 35 feet wide or narrower, and a special exception on wider lots. The app does not measure lot width, so that case stays "needs special approval (P/S)."

A, S, and C are labeled needs special approval, not a variance. Standards cited with the table are §911.04A.69, §911.04A.69A, and §911.04A.85. SP districts, planned unit developments, public-realm districts, and Mount Oliver Borough are not columns in this table. Those parcels say to check with the City. They are not marked prohibited. `needs_expert_review` stays true. This is decision support, not a determination of what may be built. Overlays are not applied.

**Flood, slope, and mines.** These are three different maps with different update cycles. In this pull, steep slopes touch about 57% of Hazelwood parcels and about 15% of Lawrenceville parcels. Undermined areas show up on about 13% of Hazelwood parcels and on none of these Lawrenceville parcels. Mapped FEMA zones run the other way: about 6% of Lawrenceville parcels and under 1% of Hazelwood parcels. They are not combined into a single "Hazelwood is riskier" fact. Absence from the FEMA query is not a survey. Slope overlap is not a landslide. Mine overlap is not a safety determination.

**Transit schedule versus the street.** Trip counts are the GTFS schedule for one weekday. They are not delay, crowding, or a sidewalk. The drop-a-building ring uses the same stops, written to `stops.geojson` (name, weekday trip count, route ids). A stop is inside the ring when it is within 800 meters of the parcel point. The parcel score's trip count is still the 400 meter figure. Both are scheduled service.

**List URL versus the file we could download.** Where the organizers' URL 404'd or the HTML page blocked this client, `sources.json` keeps the list URL and records `access_url` separately.

## Names of people

Owner names, change-notice mailing addresses, and buyer, seller, grantor, and grantee names are not requested and not written. The City-owned and condemned-property resources have an `owner` column, and the delinquency resource has `billing_city` and dollar amounts. None of those are requested: the WPRDC dump URLs name their fields, and the pipeline refuses a dump whose header contains a forbidden word. `billing` is now on the forbidden list. The parcel file keeps only booleans and status codes from these layers. The assessment query asks only for parcel id, site address, class, use, lot area, year built, living area, and sale date, price, and validation code. `pipeline/config.py` refuses a parcel file whose keys contain `owner`, `changenotice`, `mailing`, `buyer`, `seller`, `grantor`, or `grantee`.

The site address stays, because the map is about the parcel. The last valid sale year and price stay on the parcel, without the parties. Neighborhood scores use the median and the count, not a list of transactions. Do not join this file back to an owner roll or a deed-party file for a public demo.
