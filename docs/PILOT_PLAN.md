# Playhouse

Pittsburgh Planning Studio

by Hack the House

# Pilot plan (proposal, not agreed)

A two-week pilot to find out whether Playhouse helps a real team prepare a better housing-site shortlist, and where it is wrong. No partner has agreed; names below are roles, not commitments.

## Participants

- One community development corporation working in Hazelwood or Lawrenceville (2–3 staff who prepare site shortlists).
- One qualified zoning reviewer (planner or zoning attorney) to check the §911.02 readings.
- The team (Tejas Chaudhari, Chris Severns, Yoon Yik Ng) to run sessions and log findings.

## Week 1: review

1. Kickoff (1 hour): the CDC names 8–12 real sites it is already considering or has rejected, and the questions it asks about them.
2. The zoning reviewer checks the tool's §911.02 readings for those sites (permission by unit count, overlays, lot-width rules). Every disagreement is logged.
3. CDC staff use Find Sites and the comparison to build their own shortlist, thinking aloud; the team observes and does not coach.

## Week 2: compare and measure

1. Compare the tool's shortlist and explanations with the CDC's own judgment, site by site. Log each disagreement as: data error, zoning error, missing factor, weighting choice, or explanation problem.
2. Measure time to prepare a two-scenario brief with and without the tool (the same staff on different sites).
3. Debrief: which next steps in the brief were useful, which were missing, and what the team must never rely on it for.

## What we measure (targets set with participants, not in advance)

- Consequential errors: wrong permission readings, missing hazards, misleading explanations (count, and severity by the reviewer's judgment).
- Agreement between the tool's top options and practitioner choices, with reasons for disagreements.
- Time to a usable brief.
- Checks practitioners needed that the tool does not do.

## Safeguards

- Decision support only; no site is acquired, proposed, or published because of the pilot.
- No personal data beyond what the public layers already hold; no owner names or debt amounts.
- Findings are shared back with the participants before any public use.

## Roadmap after the pilot (not built)

Rehab, reuse, and no-change baselines; reviewed dimensional envelopes and unit ranges; affordability, tenure, and subsidy scenarios; real pedestrian routes and jobs/services access; a community-priority workshop to replace the illustrative priorities; reviewed corrections with source refresh and versioning; shared (not browser-only) feedback storage; portfolio comparisons across several sites.
